107: \item  \textbf{The feedback-driven offloading policy jointly considers latency and estimated power through a mathematically defined threshold update law:} that controls where inference runs. After each cycle, NSSON logs the end-to-end latency $L_{\text{e2e}}$ and total modeled power $P_{\text{total}}$ into \texttt{latency\_log.csv} and \texttt{power\_log.csv}, and the feedback module takes the last $N$ entries to compute sliding-window averages $\bar{L}$ and $\bar{P}$. These averages are then used in the update rule $\theta(t) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\theta_0 - \alpha_L \bar{L} - \alpha_P \bar{P}, 0.30, 0.90\right)$, so that large recent latency or modeled energy lowers $\theta(t)$, thereby favoring local execution and reducing the offload ratio. Conversely, when recent latency and modeled energy decrease, $\theta(t)$ rises toward its base value, increasing the offload ratio. When recent latency and power are small, the subtraction term shrinks, $\theta(t)$ moves back toward its base value $\theta_0$, and more samples are executed locally, reducing network usage and transfer energy. Because the offload decision $D(s,t)$ compares confidence $C(s)$ against the current $\theta(t)$, every update of the threshold immediately changes the placement of subsequent inferences. The empirical offload ratio $\phi_{\text{offload}}$ and local ratio $\phi_{\text{local}}$ derived from these decisions are used to study how different feedback settings move the system across latency–energy–QoS trade-offs. Experiments show that this closed-loop policy achieves substantial reductions in average latency and power versus always-local execution while maintaining stable threshold dynamics, illustrating a novel, jointly latency–power-aware way of adapting inference placement in AIoT systems.

609: \subsection{Threshold-Sweep Validation}
610: \label{sec:thresholdsweep}
612: To validate the direction of the adaptive policy, we evaluated the offload ratio across a fixed sweep of confidence thresholds. Consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision}, the offload ratio increases monotonically with $\theta(t)$: a higher threshold classifies more samples as low-confidence and therefore offloads them. Conversely, lowering $\theta(t)$ increases the proportion of locally executed samples. This monotonic relationship confirms that the feedback law moves the system in the intended direction: increased recent latency or modeled energy reduces $\theta(t)$ and hence reduces offloading, whereas improved recent conditions raise $\theta(t)$ and increase offloading.

622: The NSSON controller is implemented as a Ryu OpenFlow~1.3 application. Each inference cycle writes a telemetry record containing a globally unique \texttt{sample\_id}, model confidence, offload decision, threshold, and Unix timestamp. The host embeds the same \texttt{sample\_id} in the application-layer payload of the corresponding inference flow. The controller extracts this identifier at each \texttt{PacketIn} event and correlates it with the telemetry entry using the five-tuple $(\text{src MAC}, \text{dst MAC}, \text{src port}, \text{dst port}, \texttt{sample\_id})$.
624: If a matching telemetry entry exists and is younger than a configurable freshness interval of 2\,s, the controller installs a flow rule according to the recorded decision. For an offload decision, it installs a priority-100 rule forwarding the flow toward the edge-server interface. For a local decision, it installs a priority-10 best-effort rule; no edge-server route is installed for locally executed inference.
626: If no matching telemetry entry exists, or the newest matching entry exceeds the freshness interval, the controller applies a conservative default best-effort rule and records the event as \texttt{unmatched\_or\_stale}. This prevents a stale decision belonging to another UAV or inference sample from being applied to the current flow. Concurrent flows are disambiguated by the complete five-tuple and unique \texttt{sample\_id}; the controller does not rely solely on the most recent global telemetry entry.
